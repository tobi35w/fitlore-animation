Marcus paced circles around his coffee table at midnight, desperate for twenty-six hundred more steps.
His smart watch flashed a mocking red ring.
Seven thousand was not enough; only the holy ten thousand could save him.
He marched past his sleeping cat.
He did frantic high knees while brushing his teeth.
At midnight, Marcus stubbed his toe and collapsed onto the rug.
Luckily, his tracker registered the crash as two extra steps.
Unluckily, the entire target was invented by a clock salesman.
You see, ten thousand was never a medical prescription.
In nineteen sixty-five, a Japanese company launched the Manpo-kei pedometer.
They chose ten thousand because the Japanese character looks like a walking person.
It was an advertising gimmick, not biology.
Harvard studies show longevity benefits level off around seventy-five hundred steps.
Pushing past that doesn't add years; it just steals your sleep.
So Marcus silenced his watch, smiled at his cat, and took four steps into bed.
